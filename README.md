## Abstract

This project focuses on applying machine learning-based predictive analytics to improve decision-making across the supply chain. Traditional supply chain management relies on static rules and historical averages, which struggle to adapt to real-time demand shifts, supplier disruptions, and transportation delays. This project evaluates predictive models across key supply chain functions—demand forecasting, inventory optimization, and supplier risk scoring—using historical transactional and logistics data. Multiple machine learning models, including Linear/Time-Series Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and LSTM, are trained and compared based on their predictive accuracy. Model performance is evaluated using metrics such as MAPE, RMSE, and F1-score, considering both forecasting accuracy and computational efficiency. Finally, a comparative framework evaluates accuracy, robustness, and practical applicability to recommend the most suitable ML approach for proactive supply chain decision-making.

## Objectives

* To develop machine learning models for forecasting demand and predicting supply chain disruptions from historical business data.
* To integrate multiple model families (tree-based, boosting, and sequence-based) to compare predictive performance across supply chain tasks.
* To optimize inventory and supplier decisions using data-driven, model-generated predictions rather than static rules.
* To evaluate and compare model performance and practical effectiveness to identify the most suitable ML approach for supply chain optimization.
